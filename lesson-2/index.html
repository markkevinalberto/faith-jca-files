Basic Rights of Employees

According to P.D. 442, the Labor Code of the Philippines

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

P.D. 442: Why It Was Established

Safeguards

Filipino workers’ rights, equity in the workplace, and social justice between employers and employees

Guides

Positive working relationships, with clear guidelines for working hours, pay, benefits, and job security

Balances

Business expansion and worker welfare, to keep industrial harmony and productivity

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Normal Hours of Work and Overtime

12345678+25%or more
8 hours a day: normal hours of workBeyond 8 hours
Normal hours

Employees should normally work 8 hours a day. Work beyond 8 hours is overtime, which must be paid extra.

Overtime pay

At least 25% more than the regular hourly rate; at least 30% more on a holiday or rest day.

Why it matters

Workers are not overworked and are paid fairly for extra work.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Night Shift, Rest Days and Holidays

Night Shift Differential+10%

Employees working between 10 PM and 6 AM must get extra pay, not less than 10% of their hourly rate.

Night workers deserve added compensation.

Rest Day1 a week

Workers must have at least one rest day every week. Employers should respect the employee’s religious beliefs when setting rest days.

Rest days let workers recover and spend time with their families.

HolidaysDouble pay

Employees are paid on regular holidays even if they don’t work. If they work during a holiday, they get double pay.

It protects the right to rest and rewards those who work.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Leave and Special Protection

Service Incentive Leave5 days

Workers who have served for at least 1 year get 5 days of paid leave per year.

A chance to rest or attend to personal matters.

Women and MinorsBelow 15

Special protection for women and minors. Children below 15 are not allowed to work, except in family businesses under safe conditions.

Women and young workers are treated fairly and protected from abuse.

Benefits for Special GroupsMore rights

Special laws give additional rights to women (e.g., maternity leave), minors, and persons with disabilities.

Some groups need more protection in the workplace.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Wages: Paid Right and On Time

Wages₱ only

Paid in legal tender (Philippine pesos), not in products or promises. Employers must give payslips showing salary and deductions.

This guarantees fairness and transparency.

Payment of Wages2× a month

At least once every two weeks or twice a month, paid directly to the worker at or near the workplace.

Employees receive their earnings regularly and conveniently.

Prohibition of DeductionsBy law only

No deductions from wages unless allowed by law (e.g., taxes, SSS, Pag-IBIG, PhilHealth contributions).

It prevents unfair wage cuts and protects employees’ income.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2
In summary · Book Three of the Labor Code

Book Three of the Labor Code makes sure employees have fair working hours, proper pay, rest days, holidays, leave benefits, and protection against abuse. It also ensures that wages are paid correctly and on time.

The different types of employment appointments in government service

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Four Types of Appointment

Government service
Permanent

For a person who fully meets all qualification standards required by law

Temporary

When no qualified applicant with eligibility is available; up to 12 months

Coterminous

Primarily confidential in nature, or tied to the lifespan of the agency

Substitute

While the regular employee is temporarily unable to perform duties

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Permanent and Temporary

Appointment examples
Permanent

For a person who fully meets all qualification standards: eligibility, education, training, and experience.

Example

Maria Bella became a Permanent Senior Education Program Specialist after meeting all the CSC qualification standards.

Temporary

When no qualified applicant with eligibility is available; up to 12 months, renewable only under certain conditions. Not for licensed positions, unless justified.

Example

A town office needs an Administrative Aide I but no applicant has Civil Service eligibility, so a qualified college graduate gets a Temporary Appointment.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Coterminous and Substitute

Appointment examples
Coterminous

Primarily confidential in nature, or tied to the lifespan of the agency.

Example

The mayor’s Private Secretary serves at the mayor’s pleasure; when the mayor’s term ends or the mayor is replaced, the appointment ends too.

Substitute

While the regular employee is temporarily unable to work (leave, suspension, scholarship, secondment); ends when the incumbent returns.

Example

Ms. Tadena, an LET passer, takes Mrs. Sampana’s Grade 4 class during her 105-day maternity leave (Sept. 1–Dec. 15); no tenure.

Flexible Work Arrangements

In government and in private companies

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Flexible Work in Government

CSC Resolution No. 2200209

The Civil Service Commission, through Resolution No. 2200209, institutionalizes flexible work arrangements (FWAs) in government to ensure the continuous delivery of public service while safeguarding the health, safety, and welfare of employees.

Who is covered

All government officials and employees, regardless of employment status, across:

AgenciesLGUsGOCCs with original chartersSUCs
LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

FWAs the Resolution Allows

Government
Flexiplace

Work away from the office (home, satellite office), with approval

Compressed Workweek

40 hours in four days or less, instead of five

Skeleton Workforce

Only the minimum staff report, to keep operations going

Work Shifting

Work in shifts for 24/7 services or staggered presence

Flexitime

Arrive and leave between 7 AM and 7 PM; 40 hours a week

Combination

Mix two or more FWAs (e.g., skeleton workforce + work from home)

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Flexible Work in Private Companies

Besides telecommuting (R.A. No. 11165), private companies also use:

Compressed Workweek

Longer hours on fewer days, same total weekly hours (e.g., four 10-hour days instead of five 8-hour days).

Flexitime

Employees set their start and end times within a core period, as long as they complete the required hours.

Job Sharing

Two or more employees share one full-time position, splitting the hours and responsibilities.

Reduced Workdays or Rotation

In downturns or low demand, fewer workdays or rotating shifts to avoid retrenchments.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Total Pay in the Government

Under the TCF

The total payment to an employee for services rendered is composed of:

1Basic Salaries (with Step Increments)

The fixed monthly pay; may increase through step increments based on performance or length of service.

2Standard Allowances and Benefits

Regular benefits for all qualified employees (e.g., PERA, ACA, health benefits).

3Specific Purpose Allowances and Benefits

Additional pay or benefits for specific needs or situations.

4Incentives

Rewards or bonuses based on performance, productivity, or recognition programs.

Five Work-Life Approaches

Balance · Integration · Harmony · Fit · Negotiation

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Five Ways to Manage Work and Life

Work-Life Balance

Keep work and personal time separate.

Work-Life Integration

Work and personal life coexist harmoniously.

Work-Life Harmony

Blend work into life for happiness at home and at work.

Work-Life Fit

Align work with your personal priorities and lifestyle.

Work-Life Negotiation

Talk with your employer to set schedules and commitments.

LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Work-Life Balance and Integration

WorkLife
Work-Life Balance

A structured separation between work and personal time, so work does not dominate life.

Common principles
  • Fixed hours: no emails or calls after a set time
  • Uninterrupted personal time for family, hobbies, and rest
  • Structured schedules with little spillover
Work-Life Integration

Work and personal life are interconnected and coexist; days are structured to meet both.

Example
  • EarlyElsa starts with emails
  • 7:30–8:30 AMSchool prep
  • 9 AM–3 PMMeetings, core hours
  • 3 PMPicks up her kids; works as needed
  • After bedtimeWraps up tasks
LCS_3RD QTR_LESSON 2

Work-Life Harmony and Fit

WorkLife
Work-Life Harmony

Blending work into life to be happy at home and at work: not strict separation, but prioritizing well-being.

Example

Mina walks with her kids each morning, stays focused at work, takes breaks for family or self-care, and uses flexible hours and remote work to adjust.

Work-Life Fit

Aligning work with your personal priorities and lifestyle: an adaptable blend that works best for you.

Example
  • 7 AMRoem, a single dad, starts work as a store supervisor
  • 3 PMFinishes his shift
  • After schoolThere for his kids
  • EveningWorks later, if needed
